## Proactive Technical Support Engineer

**Gutensite | Remote | March 2018 – January 2021**

Gutensite was a CMS platform provider offering website building and hosting services to small and medium businesses, mainly real estate agents and churches. Most of our customers were not technical. Many could barely handle email, so every interaction had to make them feel safe with technology they didn't understand.

I provided front-line technical support for customer websites, troubleshooting HTML, CSS, and DNS issues, and configured and customized sites using PHP, JavaScript, and AJAX. I also handled all customer communication, including pre-sales calls, which meant I was the voice of the company from a prospect's first question through every ticket after they signed.

### A few things I'm proud of

#### Proving what the numbers actually said

After my probationary period, my resolution stats looked about 30% lower than my counterpart's. I spent a month working faster, and the numbers didn't move. So I went into the tickets themselves.

My counterpart started an hour before me. Overnight, we'd get a batch of vendor announcement emails that came in as tickets and just needed to be closed. He was closing them first thing every morning, and each one counted as a resolution.

I built a Zendesk report comparing our raw numbers against numbers with the vendor tickets filtered out. With the noise removed, the picture flipped: I was resolving 30% more actual customer issues. Leadership tested the report for a month looking for holes. They didn't find any.

The lesson I took with me: a metric is only as honest as what it counts. Before you trust a number, look at what's inside it.

#### Online giving for churches, without a new feature

During the pandemic, churches moved services online, but there was no longer a collection plate going around. One church asked if they could accept tithes through their Gutensite site. The platform didn't support payments.

I researched it and found that customers could embed a PayPal button at no cost to Gutensite. Then I wrote a Knowledge Base article for it, with screenshots and step-by-step instructions written for people who were nervous about clicking the wrong thing. I included how to change the button text from "Pay" to "Tithe," because that was the next question I knew they'd ask.

Other churches found the article and set up online giving on their own, without opening a ticket. A well-written doc is a ticket that never gets opened.

#### Honest pre-sales

On sales calls, I asked prospects what they actually needed the site to do, then told them plainly where Gutensite was a great fit and where it wasn't. Some didn't sign. The ones who did knew exactly what they were getting, which meant fewer surprises, fewer angry tickets, and customers who trusted us when something went wrong.

#### Coaching customers to better sites

I worked with frequent callers to improve how their sites looked and read, from layout to photos. Small changes, like replacing a blurry cropped phone selfie with a real headshot, made a real difference in how their business came across.
