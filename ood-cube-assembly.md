# Assembling a Reinforced Wood Box

This is a response to an interview question asking me to explain how to assemble a cube from six wooden boards using only wood glue. The task specifies that the finished assembly must survive a 10 cm drop test.

A woodworker wrote these instructions. Buckle up.

---

## A Note on the Geometry

The provided pieces cannot form a true cube without modification. A cube requires all six faces to be identical squares. The two board sizes given -- four pieces at 10 x 10 x 2 cm and two pieces at 12 x 12 x 2 cm -- produce mismatched face sizes regardless of assembly orientation.

To build a structurally sound 12 cm box with flush exterior faces, the four wall pieces must be trimmed:

- **Four wall pieces:** Cut to 10 cm x 8 cm x 2 cm
- **Two cap pieces:** Remain at 12 cm x 12 cm x 2 cm

The math: 8 cm wall width + 2 cm cap thickness + 2 cm cap thickness = 12 cm on all exterior faces.

The assessment does not prohibit cutting, and the drop test will punish a poorly fitted assembly before the glue even has a chance to fail.

---

## Materials

- Four wall pieces: 10 x 8 x 2 cm (trimmed from the 10 x 10 x 2 cm boards)
- Two cap pieces: 12 x 12 x 2 cm
- Titebond III Ultimate wood glue (see note below)
- Four 90-degree corner clamps (clamping squares)
- Four bar clamps
- Cauls (scrap wood scraps, at least 12 cm long)
- Silicone glue brush
- Pencil
- Shop towels (damp)
- Flat, stable work surface

> **Why Titebond III?** The assessment links to Elmer's Carpenter's Wood Glue, which is fine for household repairs. For an impact test, you want Titebond III Ultimate. It has a 10-minute open time -- enough working time to assemble all four walls before the first joint begins to set -- and it reaches full cure strength in 24 hours. A joint that feels solid at one hour can still fail under impact. Do not rush this.

All wood must be S4S (Surfaced 4 Sides): planed smooth on both faces, ripped square on both edges.

---

## Understanding Wood Surfaces

Wood has three distinct surface types. Joint strength depends entirely on which surfaces you are bonding.

| Surface | Description | Glue Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **Face grain** | The wide, flat face of the board | Excellent |
| **Edge grain** | The long, narrow side of the board | Very good |
| **End grain** | The cut end of the board; exposed fiber tips | Poor |

End grain behaves like a bundle of drinking straws with the open ends facing you. Glue applied to end grain is absorbed into the fibers rather than bonding to the surface. No matter how much you apply, an end grain joint will always be the weakest point in the assembly. The drop test is specifically designed to fail assemblies that rely on end grain joints.

The assembly method below is designed to minimize end grain exposure at every corner.

---

## Understanding Glue Timing

Two timing concepts matter and are easy to confuse.

- **Open time:** How long the glue stays workable after application. For Titebond III, this is approximately 10 minutes. You must have all four walls assembled and clamped before the first joint reaches this threshold.
- **Clamp time:** How long the joint must remain clamped before it can be safely handled. For Titebond III, this is 40 to 50 minutes. The glue is not fully cured at this point -- it has simply set enough to hold its shape.
- **Cure time:** Full cure takes 24 hours. Do not perform the drop test before this window has elapsed.

---

## Labeling the Pieces

Label each piece on the surface that will face the interior of the finished box. Pencil marks stay hidden and do not interfere with glue joints.

- **W1, W2, W3, W4** -- label on the inward-facing flat surface
- **P5 (lid)** -- label on the bottom face, which will face inward
- **P6 (base)** -- label on the top face, which will face inward

---

## Step 1: Size the End Grain

Before any assembly begins, the end grain on all four wall pieces must be sealed. This process is called "sizing."

1. Using the silicone brush, apply a thin, even coat of Titebond III to the end grain of each wall piece.
2. Wait 2 to 3 minutes. The wood will visibly absorb the glue; the surface will shift from wet to matte or tacky. This seals the fiber tips and prevents the bonding coat from being absorbed instead of bonding.

Do not skip this step. An unsealed end grain joint will fail.

---

## Step 2: Build the First L-Shape (W1 and W2)

The four walls are assembled using a **pinwheel (or "chasing") butt joint** pattern. Each board's end grain butts against the face grain of the next board, rotating consistently around the assembly. This produces uniform exterior dimensions on all four sides.

The walls are built in two L-shapes, then joined.

1. Apply a generous coat of Titebond III to the sized end grain of W1.
2. Press the glued end of W1 firmly against the face of W2 at a 90-degree angle. The outer edge of W1 should be flush with the outer edge of W2.
3. Place a clamping square on each interior corner and tighten.
4. Wipe any squeeze-out immediately with a damp shop towel.
5. Set aside. Leave clamped for at least 10 minutes.

---

## Step 3: Build the Second L-Shape (W3 and W4)

Repeat Step 2 with W3 and W4, following the same pinwheel orientation.

Set aside. Leave clamped for at least 10 minutes.

---

## Step 4: Join the Two L-Shapes

Once both L-shapes have tacked up and can be handled without shifting:

1. Remove the clamping squares.
2. Do a dry fit: press the two L-shapes together and confirm that the exterior footprint measures exactly 12 x 12 cm. Use P5 or P6 as a reference template if needed.
3. Apply a bonding coat of Titebond III to the exposed end grain of each L-shape.
4. Press the two L-shapes together, maintaining the pinwheel pattern.
5. Place clamping squares at both sides of each of the two new corners. Tighten all four.
6. Wipe any squeeze-out with a damp shop towel.
7. Leave clamped for at least 10 minutes before moving on.

---

## Step 5: Attach the Base (P6)

1. Run a consistent 3 mm bead of Titebond III along the entire bottom edge of the wall assembly. Do not skip the corners -- the glue must bridge the joints where wall pieces meet.
2. Place P6 onto the glued edge, aligning all outer faces flush with the wall exterior.
3. Place cauls (scrap wood) between the bar clamps and the cap face to distribute clamping pressure evenly and prevent marring.
4. Apply bar clamps vertically.
5. Leave clamped for at least 10 minutes.

---

## Step 6: Attach the Lid (P5)

1. Remove the bar clamps from the base.
2. Flip the assembly so the open top is facing up.
3. Run a consistent 3 mm bead of Titebond III along the entire top edge of the wall assembly, bridging all corner joints.
4. Place P5 onto the glued edge, flush with the wall exterior on all sides.
5. Place cauls between the bar clamps and the cap face.
6. Apply bar clamps vertically.
7. Leave clamped for at least 10 minutes before removing.

---

## Step 7: Cure and Test

Set the assembly on a flat surface and do not disturb it for **24 hours**.

After full cure, perform the drop test: hold the box at a height of 10 cm and release. The assembly should survive intact.

---

> **Why it holds:** Every corner in this assembly places the end grain of one board against the face grain of the next. The pinwheel pattern distributes this across all four walls symmetrically, so no single joint absorbs a disproportionate share of impact load. The cap pieces bear drop force across their full face grain, which is the strongest bonding surface available. With Titebond III at full cure, the glue bond at those face joints is stronger than the wood itself.
