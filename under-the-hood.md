## Under the Hood

I think in systems. When a customer reports a problem, my first instinct isn't to jump to a solution. It's to understand the boundaries of the problem first. What's working? What isn't? What changed? A well-scoped problem is halfway solved.

### Communication

I write the way I troubleshoot: clearly, specifically, and without unnecessary complexity. When I escalate to engineering, everything is scoped, documented, and actionable. When I write for customers, I assume they're intelligent people who are busy and frustrated, not people who need to be talked down to.

I built AI workflows at Axonius using deliberately simple prompts, one to three sentences, that consistently outperformed my colleagues' more elaborate versions. The lesson wasn't about AI. It was the same lesson I keep learning in every context: clarity scales better than complexity.

### Documentation

I've authored more than 30 internal and external Knowledge Base articles. I don't write docs because it's part of the job. I write them because every good doc is a problem that doesn't come back.

### Collaboration

I've spent my career at the boundary between customers and engineering, translating in both directions. Customers don't speak engineer. Engineers don't always speak customer. That translation layer is where I live, and I'm good at it.
