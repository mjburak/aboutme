## Triage

I often work in high-volume environments where my backlog can grow to 50 tickets, or more.  Sometimes, the tickets come in too fast to handle them one at a time, so I've learned to triage.  Every ticket gets sized up on two fronts:

1.  Technical Difficulty
2.  Political Environment

I can read a ticket and know how complex of an issue it is likely to be, and therefore how much time it will take.  

I also know to ask a customer if their issue is a blocker on their end, or if they're getting pressure from their leadership about it.  I also check to see if an account is up for renewal.  That will give me a sense of the political landscape.  

With all of this knowledge, I know which tickets take top priority.

And, if I have more than one ticket that's equal on both the technical and political fronts, then I "follow the money".  I'll pull Salesforce to see which account carries more revenue and let that break the tie.

But, I don't focus on just one ticket at a time.  I do round-robbin.  Once I reach some sort of a stopping point with one ticket, e.g., waiting for a reply from the customer, waiting on engineering, etc., then I move on to the next. 

The triage gets reevaluated in real time, as things change.

I also don't work in isolation.  For high-severity, high-priority tickets, I loop in sales, the TAM, and leadership as needed.  Plus, the customers get frequent updates on the status of their issue.  I don't want anyone to be surprised.
